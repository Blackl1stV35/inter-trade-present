 Stegra says needs extra capital for giant green steel plant, appoints Wallenberg executive as new CEO
 Stegra said its largest shareholders have expressed an interest in helping secure the additional funding.

 Swedish green steel startup Stegra today said
it would need extra capital to finish construction of a giant green steel
plant, as it appointed an executive with a key investor as its new CEO.
Stegra said additional capital will be needed for Europe's first hydrogen-fuelled steel plant, located in Boden, Sweden, as the estimated costs of completing it are “significantly higher” than
expected in June this year, following a review involving external advisors. 
It said the cost increases are mainly due to substantial
ramp-up costs following the prolonged scaling back of work earlier this year,
as well as inflation. 
According to sources cited by the publication
Affärsvärlden, at least SEK 10 billion, around €880m, is needed to complete the plant. Stegra
declined to comment.
Leif Johansson, Stegra chair, said: "The financing need is greater than we had hoped, but we have a robust
plan to complete the project and have identified greater opportunities for
outsourcing and partnerships than we previously thought. 
“If anything, our belief in Stegra and in the business
opportunities once the steel plant is complete is stronger today than when we
came on board in June, and we are now working constructively with all of the
company’s stakeholders to find a solution to the financing need.”
In April, Stegra
secured €1.4bn in rescue funding from a consortium led by the prominent
Swedish Wallenberg family for the project.
The consortium also includes Temasek and IMAS,
with the consortium taking “a leading position in Stegra". It was also supported by Stegra’s existing
shareholders, including Altor, as well as Hy24 and Just Climate.
Stegra today said its largest shareholders
have “expressed a positive view on taking part in the work to
secure the capital required to complete the project”.
The board has also appointed former Saab CEO Håkan Buskhe as
its new CEO, replacing Henrik Henriksson. Buskhe, who is head of special investments at Wallenberg Investments and a Stegra board member, was CEO and president of Saab between 2010 and 2019.
