 Stegra again requires additional funding for the construction of a green plant 
 Читайте українськоюЧитайте на русском
Halina Yermolenko 
 According to the Swedish press, the increase in expenditure amounts to at least 10 billion kronor
 The Swedish start-up Stegra will need additional funding running into billions to complete the construction of a large-scale steelworks in Boden, reports EFN.
 The summer funding round, as the business channel notes, turned out to be merely a brief period of optimism for the company.
 Specifically, on 30 September, Stegra announced that it was dismissing its CEO, Henrik Henriksson — a key figure in the project since its launch in 2021. From 1 November 2026, Håkan Buskhe will take over the role. Henriksson will remain with the company as an adviser. In addition, the start-up acknowledged that it needs to raise funds again, without specifying a particular amount.
 It is also noted that, since the arrival of the new group of owners and the new board of directors in June, a further in-depth review of the company and the project in Boden has been carried out with the support of external consultants. This analysis shows, in particular, that additional capital is required to complete the project, as the estimated costs significantly exceed those forecast in the summer.
 According to the publication Affärsvärlden, the increase in expenditure amounts to at least 10 billion Swedish kronor, bringing the total capital raised by the company (through loans and investments) to around 100 billion kronor.
 Christian Kopfer, a commodities analyst at the brokerage firm Arctic Securities, quoted by EFN, has cast doubt on the effectiveness of the project’s management, given that Stegra is once again having to raise funds.
 Stegra is implementing one of Europe’s largest projects to decarbonise the steel industry. The company plans to produce steel using hydrogen and renewable energy, thereby reducing carbon emissions compared with traditional blast furnace production. The start-up is building a large-scale plant in Boden to produce environmentally friendly steel. It is to comprise an electrolyser, a direct reduction unit, two electric arc furnaces, as well as cold rolling and finishing shops.
 The plan was to carry out construction work costing 25 billion kroner, with production scheduled to begin in 2024.
 Since then, the funding requirements have risen sharply, and the project’s timetable has been postponed several times. According to Stegra’s latest statement, production will not begin until early 2027, but following a funding round in April, the company announced that the timeline is being revised.
 It should be recalled that in June, Stegra announced the completion of a €1.4 billion ($1.6 billion) funding round, alongside a change in its board of directors.
