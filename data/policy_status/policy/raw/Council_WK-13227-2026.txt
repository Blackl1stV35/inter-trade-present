Guidance for discussions:
Following the presentations by the Commission of the proposals for the revision of ETS1 on 20 and
22 July, delegations were requested to submit written comments by 24 August to inform discussions
at subsequent WPEs. Delegations were advised that the Presidency wish to organise discussions
thematically in blocks.
On 4 September, the first block for discussion will be Block 1.A Ambition/CAP. This covers proposals
regarding the adjustment of the linear reduction factor, the use of international carbon credits, and
the use of permanent carbon removals.
The second part of the day will cover Block 1.B Free Allocation, which covers benchmarks,
conditionality, and exports/CBAM.
The Commission will present these elements of the proposal to the Working Party. This will include
responding to questions by Member States received from delegations in writing compiled in a
document found on the Delegates Portal (Ref: WK 13109/26).
The text below summarises the proposal for information and is not intended to give a full detailed
explanation of the provisions.

Part 1 ­ Block 1.A Ambition/Cap

1. Linear Reduction Factor

The Commission has proposed to make amendments (under Article 9) that will slow the annual
reduction of the overall cap on allowances and to continue to issue allowances into the 2040s
(current legislation sees allowance issuance cease c. 2039). This involves decreasing the linear
reduction factor (LRF) to (i) align with the Union's emission reduction target of -90% by 2040 as
compared to 1990 levels; and (ii) take account of the European Climate Law provisions regarding the
use of high-quality international credits as contribution towards the EU 2040 climate target (starting
in 2036). The Commission has proposed:

    · to adjust the LRF from 4.4% currently to 3.7% from 2031 ­ 2035 and 1.7% from 2036. If high-
         quality international credits are not available, the LRF would revert to 2.7% from 2036. The
         LRF would apply to new municipal waste incineration allowances from 2034 (further
         information below).

    · the LRF would not apply to the increase of allowances for the purchase of domestic
         permanent carbon removal units (Article 9c (7)) and would no longer be applied to
         allocations to new entrants

2. International Credits

The Commission proposes (under new Article 9b) that up-to 260Mt of international credits may be
purchased to contribute to EU ETS emissions reductions targets from 2036-2040. These will be
funded by the auctioning of up-to 260 million allowances, purchased centrally by the Commission,
and their use reflected in the LRF adjustments described above.

The Commission proposes that such credits must be high quality, high integrity and cost-effective,
and the Commission proposes to publish a report assessing the availability of such credits by 31
January 2033. If the number of available credits is deemed to be insufficient, the LRF for 2036
onwards will automatically readjust to 2.7%. Unused allowances would be allocated to the Industrial
Decarbonisation Bank.

3. Domestic Permanent Carbon Removals

The Commission proposes to incorporate domestic carbon removals into the EU ETS under a new
Article 9c. The Commission proposes a centralised purchasing model, with the intention to auction
250 million allowances from 2031 to 2040 to generate revenue for the purchasing of an equivalent
amount of domestic permanent removal units (from domestic permanent biogenic emissions
capture with carbon storage (BioCCS) and Direct Air Carbon Capture and Storage (DACCS)

technologies certified under the EU Carbon Removal Certification Framework). A reserve of an
additional 10 million allowances can be auctioned in the event that the allocated allowances are
insufficient. The cap will be increased by the number of these new allowances meaning the amount
of removals does not impact the LRF.

The proposal includes a review clause for assessment in December 2034 covering progress of
purchasing, the transition from Commission management to full private-operator integration and
use, and to further examine the potential role of nature-based removals and carbon farming
sequestration units under the CRCF.

Article 14 (1a) also allows the Commission to adopt implementing acts which would allow operators,
aircraft operators and shipping companies to reduce their ETS obligations with the use of their own
CRCF certified BioCCS carbon removals. This compensation mechanism (i) cannot generate
negative emissions; and (ii) requires an adjustment mechanism for the purchase of carbon removals
to prevent total emissions space from increasing beyond what is intended.

4. Cap Increase with regard to scope extension

It is proposed that a proportional increase to the cap will take place to facilitate the proposed
inclusion of additional emissions in the system from the inclusion of municipal waste incineration,
additional flights and additional shipping journeys. Therefore, the discussion of the related cap
increase will be incorporated in the discussion of scope extension under blocks 2.A Aviation, 2.B
Maritime and 2.C Waste.

Part 2 ­ Block 1.B Free allocation

Further to its assessment of the risk of carbon leakage, the Commission proposes to extend the
allocation of free allowances while slowing the phase-in of CBAM. The proposal also amends the
conditions that must be met by installations if they are to receive a free allocation.

1. Free Allocation Extension and Conditionality

For sectors considered at risk of carbon leakage, the Commission proposal extends free allocation
from 2030 to 2040 (Article 10b (1)).

Free allocation from 2031 would be made conditional on investment in decarbonisation (Article 10a
(3b)). The amendment requires operators to invest an amount equivalent to the value of free
allocation for the five-year period into eligible decarbonisation investments in the EU. 80% of the free
allocation would be allocated on submission of the decarbonisation investment plan, with the

                                                                                                                    3
remaining 20% allocated after confirmation that the investments were implemented at the level
expected in the EU and with significant emissions reductions realised.

This condition, based on investment in decarbonisation (Article 10a(3a)), replaces the existing
condition requiring implementation of decarbonisation actions recommended by energy audit or
certified energy management system, as well as conditionality on the least efficient installations -
above 80th percentile for relevant product benchmarks (Article 10a (1) subparagraphs 3-6).

The proposal exempts (Article 10a (3c)) the 10% most efficient installations, small installations
remaining voluntarily in the ETS, and zero emissions or low-carbon installations from the new
conditionality under Article 10a(3b/3c). Also exempt would be installations with projects supported
by the Industrial Decarbonisation Bank and Investment Booster, or the Innovation Fund when the IF
funding is disbursed or in the case of IB/IDB construction works have started. In both cases,
exemption will apply only if the amount of funding from IF, IB and IDB match at least the economic
value of 100% of FA in the relevant allocation period.

3. Benchmarks

For 2026­2030, benchmark values are updated using an annual reduction rate (ARR) subject to a
2.5% maximum or 0.3% minimum (Article 10a (2.c)). Where the ARR falls outside this range, the
minimum or maximum rate, as relevant, is applied for each year between 2008 and 2028.

For the period 2031 ­ 2035, the same methodology applies (Article 10a (2.f)). However, the
maximum ARR would be lowered from 2.5% to 2.0% while the minimum would remain at 0.3%. The
relevant ARR would be applied for each year between 2008 and 2033.

For 2036 - 2040 the same range would apply, while the relevant ARR would be applied for each year
between 2008 and 2038 (Article 10a (2.h)).

Article 10a (2a) has been added to account for the proposed 2026-2030 benchmark regulation
(COM(2026) 619), allowing for the full use of a buffer of allowances (per Article 10a (5a)) to be used
to reduce the heat and fuel benchmarks (2027-2030), while maintaining the existing auctioning share.
The buffer would be increased from 3% to 4% from 2031 to 2040 (per Article 10a(5a)).

Further to the adjustments above, up to 50 million allowances can support the Innovation Fund and
remaining allowances to support the IDB if less than 3% or 4% allowances are needed under Article
10a(5a) for the periods up to 2030 and post-2030, respectively (Article 10a(5b)).

4. Sector Specific Treatment

Sector specific heat/fuel benchmarks

The Commission proposes to provide, via delegated acts, sector-specific definitions of heat and fuel
benchmarks to include system boundaries and starting point values (Article 10a (2)). Currently heat
and fuel benchmarks apply to all sectors that are not covered by the 52 product-specific
benchmarks.

District heating

Currently Article 10b (4) subparagraphs 2-5 provides for an additional 30% free allocation to district
heating from 2026-2030 for certain MSs, subject to investment in and progress on carbon neutrality
plans. From 2031, the Proposal removes (Article10a (3a)) the reporting requirement and 30% top
up.
