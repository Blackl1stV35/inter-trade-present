# Final Deck Draft: The Steel Question

Each page below is the slide as it will appear on screen, followed by its speaker notes. The deck runs about 8 to 10 minutes plus Q&A. All figures come from the report, with policy positions as of 2 November 2026. The deck does not claim that carbon policy caused any specific change, because the report does not establish causation.

Production notes:
- Page 6 uses an animated GIF built from the Figure 2.1 model.
- Page 10 is played by three teammate agents, each answering only from the report.

## Page 1. Title

- On slide: The Steel Question
- On slide: Conditions for cleaner steel in German manufacturing
- Visual: KU logo top left, a looping GIF of a steel coil bottom right.
- Speaker notes: "We want to answer one question. How do carbon rules change the steel that German factories buy?"

## Page 2. Carbon cost lands on buyers

- On slide: EU carbon rules price emissions inside the EU and charge covered imports at the border.
- On slide: The EU Emissions Trading System (ETS) prices carbon at the producer.
- On slide: The Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) applies to listed imported goods, including iron and steel.
- Visual: a simple flow from steel mill to manufacturer, with carbon price tags on the mill and the import gate.
- Speaker notes: "The ETS prices emissions at the mill. CBAM covers listed imports. A manufacturer feels both in the price of the steel it buys."
- Source: report Section 1.

## Page 3. German steel output is falling

- On slide: German crude steel fell from 43.3 million tonnes (2017) to 34.1 million tonnes (2025), down 21.2%.
- On slide: Rolled-steel market supply fell from 40.8 million tonnes to 29.1 million tonnes over the same period.
- Visual: two large numbers side by side with a downward arrow on each.
- Speaker notes: "German crude steel fell by about a fifth in eight years. Rolled-steel supply to the market fell further."
- Source: report Section 2.3 (German Steel Federation figures).

## Page 4. Carbon policy is one of four forces

- On slide: Four forces shape German steel output and sales.
- On slide: Energy costs: industrial electricity prices.
- On slide: Demand.
- On slide: International competition.
- On slide: Carbon policy.
- On slide: The data cannot separate their effects.
- Visual: four arrows converging on a steel coil.
- Speaker notes: "We cannot say how much of the decline is due to carbon policy. Energy prices and demand matter as well."
- Source: report Sections 2.1.3 and 2.3.

## Page 5. The free-allocation countdown

- On slide: Free-allocation support runs out in 2034 under current law.
- On slide: 2026: 97.5% of the benchmark-based factor remains.
- On slide: 2030: 51.5% remains.
- On slide: 2034: zero under current law.
- On slide: The Commission proposal of 17 July 2026 would keep 15% from 2034 to 2037 and complete withdrawal in 2038.
- Visual: a timeline with a solid line for current law and a dashed branch for the proposal.
- Speaker notes: "Under current law, the shield ends in 2034. The July 2026 proposal would slow that, but only if investment follows."
- Source: report Section 1.6. These are selected milestones, not import-tax rates.

## Page 7. Direct extra-EU Chapter 72 (steel) imports fell 56.2%

- On slide: Direct extra-EU Chapter 72 (steel) imports fell 56.2%, from 3.342 million tonnes (2019) to 1.464 million tonnes (2025).
- On slide: Extra-EU means partners outside the EU-27 (2020 composition), recorded at customs.
- On slide: Direct extra-EU exports were 4.681 million tonnes in 2025.
- On slide: Direct customs records only. The origin-based decline is smaller, about 22%.
- Visual: a two-bar chart for 2019 and 2025 imports, with the 2025 export bar beside them.
- Speaker notes: "Direct imports from outside the EU fell sharply in customs records. Counted by origin, the fall is smaller, about 22%."
- Source: report Section 2.4.1. The series is product-specific and is not the CBAM liability base.

## Page 8. Companies are committing

- On slide: Three company cases: two with public aid, one memorandum.
- On slide: Salzgitter SALCOS: about EUR 1.322 billion in announced public support. An EWE green-hydrogen contract covers about 10,000 tonnes a year from 2030.
- On slide: Thyssenkrupp: approved in 2023 with up to EUR 2 billion in aid (EUR 550 million grant plus up to EUR 1.45 billion conditional).

- Visual: three cards in a row, one per company.

## Page 9. Announced is not built

- On slide: Announced steel plants face delivery dates, a stalled DRI project and job cuts.
- On slide: ArcelorMittal said on 19 June 2025 that it could not proceed with its DRI and EAF plans in Bremen and Eisenhüttenstadt.
- On slide: SALCOS plant start is planned for 2027. EWE hydrogen supply starts in 2030. The two dates are separate milestones.
- On slide: Thyssenkrupp's November 2024 plan named about 5,000 job reductions by 2030.
- Visual: two columns, "Commitments" on the left and "Risks" on the right.
- Speaker notes: "A timeline is only as strong as its weakest link. We check the whole chain, not one press release."
- Source: report Sections 2.5 and 2.6.

## Page 10. Q&A: three skeptics

- On slide: Three questions, three answers from the report.
- On slide: Steelmaker: "Why switch if the free allocation ends in 2034?"
  - Answer: Under current law the factor reaches zero in 2034. The proposal would keep 15% from 2034 to 2037, tied to investment. The report does not establish the euro cost of the ETS after 2034.
- On slide: Plant CFO: "What if the EWE hydrogen arrives late?"
  - Answer: EWE covers about 6.5% of SALCOS hydrogen needs, so a late start affects a small share. Most supply needs other arrangements, which the report does not model.
- On slide: Brussels official: "Why keep paying for a shield that ends?"
  - Answer: The report recommends a conditional extension, not protection without new capacity.
- Visual: three speech bubbles, one per persona.
- Speaker notes: Each answer is read from the slide. Any question the report cannot answer is marked "not established in the report."
- Source: report Sections 1.6, 2.5 and 3.
