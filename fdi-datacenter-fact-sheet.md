# FDI Data Centers in Thailand: Verified Fact Sheet

Research date: 3 November 2026. The first pass (workflow run wf_5213c428-34a) researched each claim live and re-checked it with an adversarial verifier. The open items were then re-checked with web searches. Those searches returned article summaries, and most primary documents were not opened, so each item below notes how far it was confirmed. Charts are pending.

Use only the claims marked usable. Do not put dropped or unverified claims on slides.

## 1. Investment and incentives (Thailand)

Usable:

- Data center investment reached 521.2 billion baht (US$16.1 billion) across 28 projects in the first half of 2025, according to the Board of Investment (BOI) as reported by w.media (6 August 2025).
- In 2025, 36 data center promotion applications worth 728 billion baht were reported (Thairath, 20 January 2026). These are applications, not approvals.
- On 11 December 2025, the BOI granted investment promotion to 11 data center projects worth over US$5.77 billion, in Bangkok, Chonburi, Rayong and Samut Prakan (BOI press release No. 179/2568, read in full). The release does not give a baht figure, so the 184,740 million baht number should not be used.
- In January 2026, the BOI board approved seven data center projects worth over 96 billion baht (Nation Thailand; search summary).
- From 1 July 2025, BOI incentives depend on two tiers, set by power usage effectiveness (PUE), water efficiency and tangible Thai benefits. High-efficiency projects receive an 8-year corporate income tax exemption and others receive 5 years, capped at 100% of investment value (Tilleke and Gibbins, 10 February 2026). The source gives no numeric PUE or water thresholds.
- Notification Por. 3/2569 (6 February 2026) revises the rules for category 8.2.1 (data centers). According to a Tilleke and Gibbins summary, training programs must reach at least ten times the project's headcount, and applicants must have binding commitments, such as agreements with schools, before using the CIT exemption. Supply-chain plans must show measurable growth in the domestic data center supply base, checked in two stages. This is law-firm reading; check the BOI gazette before quoting exact terms.

Dropped:

- The claim that 50% of management and expert staff must be Thai within 3 years was not found in any source. Drop it.
- The BOI headline of more than 220,000 jobs covers all sectors, not data centers.

Still open:

- No official BOI total for approved data center projects in 2025 was found. One law-firm figure gives 746 billion baht of data center investment within 1.87 trillion baht of total approvals, and Mondaq gives at least 458 billion baht. These differ and cannot be reconciled from secondary sources. Use only the H1 2025 figure and the application figure until the BOI annual release is read.
- No official BOI total for 2024 was found.

## 2. Electricity and water

Usable:

- Ember forecasts Thailand's data center electricity demand at 6 TWh by 2030 and 10 TWh by 2037, about 8% a year (w.media, 2 October 2025). This is a think-tank forecast.
- The Ministry's forecast peak demand for 2037 is 56,133 MW, and the plan to procure 77,407 MW of capacity is reported by The Nation. Ember's realistic scenario, which adds data center and EV demand, reaches 57,901 MW. Both are forecasts, not realized demand.
- An EGAT deputy governor reported that operators had requested about 3.5 GW against a system peak of about 40 GW, and EGAT is seeking financial safeguards for transmission and distribution costs (The Nation, 18 September 2026).
- JustPow estimates, for 34 Bangkok data centers, a demand of at least 247.2 MW and water use of about 4.43 million cubic metres a year. The estimate uses The Green Grid's water usage effectiveness metric and covers 16 of 24 operating sites with usable data (The Nation, 3 September 2026).
- The February 2024 EEC water study projects regional water demand of 800 million cubic metres by 2027 and 1 billion by 2036. About two-thirds of new data centers are planned in the EEC (Mongabay, 11 March 2026).

Dropped:

- The figure of 112,391 MW by 2037 cannot be attributed to the 2024 draft PDP. Thaiger cites it as the 2024 to 2037 plan. A Resource Solutions slide lists 112,391 MW against 77,221 MW for the 2024 draft, which suggests the figure belongs to the 2018 plan. Do not cite it.
- The "3,800 MW" warning attributed to a separate EGAT official does not appear in the article. Drop it.
- The 2024 draft is being superseded by a 2026 PDP. Say so if the PDP is mentioned.

## 3. Local opposition, jobs and governance

Usable:

- In Chonburi, near the Khlong Tamru canal, residents, fishers, a subdistrict head and activists raise water scarcity, competition with farmers, wastewater and cooling-chemical pollution, and a lack of information on resource use and environmental impact assessments (EIAs). No protest or petition is reported (Mongabay, 11 March 2026).
- In July 2026, a People's Party MP for Chonburi called for a parliamentary committee to review data center approvals. An EEC Watch researcher said EIAs are already legally required for individual projects and called for a sector-wide strategic environmental assessment (Mongabay, 27 July 2026). The EIA point is the NGO's statement.
- On 4 September 2026, a government committee chaired by the Deputy Prime Minister and Finance Minister paused 166 data center projects (49 under construction, 117 awaiting approval) while agencies draft national rules on resource use, siting, safety and economic benefit. The pause is described as temporary, and no legal basis is given (The Nation, 5 September 2026).
- Google's Chonburi facility is said to support about 100 jobs once operational. This is a company statement that is not verified. The source does not say "permanent".
- US evidence, not Thai: a 2026 Center for Global Development and Brookings brief finds that a typical US county with a data center gains about 100 to 200 permanent jobs, data processing employment rises 56% over the first decade, and wages are unchanged (Brookings, updated August 2026). A county association blog summarizes a separate Brookings study of 770 data centers, finding that permanent job creation was often well below projections. The primary study was not read.
- Georgia: the state auditors' December 2025 report said the sales-tax exemption cost $474 million in the fiscal year ending July 2025. A January 2026 correction found the economic impact had been overstated (Capitol Beat, 15 January 2026; GPB, 16 January 2026). The corrected Department of Audits summary (January 2026, read in full) confirms the figures: $474.2 million forgone in FY2025; 1,641 operations jobs ($247.0 million value added); 8,505 construction jobs ($1.0 billion value added); cost per construction job $42,646. Only 30% of Georgia's data centers are attributed to the exemption, since the other 70% would be built anyway. That attribution matters for the talk.
- Virginia: JLARC's 2024 study found that the state recovers about 48 cents of other tax revenue for each dollar of sales tax forgone under the data center exemption. Several secondary sources repeat this figure (VPM, June 2026; Hunton, August 2026). The exemption cost $1.9 billion in FY2025, according to a January 2026 JLARC report (VPM).

Dropped or unverified:

- Georgia's 1,641 figure is now confirmed by the corrected summary (see above). The linked Carl Vinson table was reported to still show the original numbers, so cite the Department of Audits summary, not the table.
- Virginia's cost of about $1.2 million per new job was not confirmed.
- Other Virginia return figures in circulation (52 cents from a watchdog report, 72 cents from a VACo summary) conflict with the 48-cent JLARC figure. Use 48 cents and cite JLARC.
- Rayong is not in the Mongabay text. Remove it unless another source supports it.

Governance:

- The Thai draft national data center framework endorsed in September 2026 is confirmed (Tilleke, 4 September 2026). The July 2025 Digital Innovation Hub aim and the 3 November 2026 Cabinet date are not confirmed.
- Whether data centers are on the mandatory EIA list was not confirmed. The search found that the category depends on a ministerial notification, that officials have said EIA requirements for standalone data centers could be added, and that many projects are reported to register as "warehouses" to avoid an EIA (Thairath; Rajah and Tann). Treat EIA coverage as an open legal point. The source for the ministerial list is the Ministry of Natural Resources and Environment notification, not yet read.
- Thai Personal Data Protection Act: the two Personal Data Protection Committee notifications under Sections 28 and 29 were published in the Government Gazette on 25 December 2023 (one source says 12 December) and took effect on 24 March 2024 (Conventus; Baker McKenzie). Sections 28 and 29 set the transfer routes, which are adequacy and safeguards such as binding corporate rules and standard clauses. One Mondaq analysis says the transfer restriction becomes operative only when the PDPC publishes a list of approved jurisdictions. Check whether that list has been published before saying the restriction is in force.

## 4. International precedents

Ireland (official CRU decision, 12 December 2025):

- New data centers must provide generation and/or storage matching their maximum import demand, and that generation must sell into the wholesale market.
- Data centers must meet at least 80% of annual demand with additional renewable generation in the Republic, on a 6-year glide path.
- EirGrid and ESB Networks had to publish a connection process by 31 March 2026. Whether they did is not verified.
- Dublin: the CRU decision ended the de facto moratorium on new connections in the greater Dublin area that had been in place since about 2021, according to RTÉ and the Irish Times. Browne Jacobson says connections remain restricted in constrained areas around Dublin, and the Irish Times reported in July 2026 that computing firms are shifting away from constrained Dublin. Environmental groups sought leave for judicial review in March 2026; the current status was not found. Present Dublin as "no longer blanket-banned, still constrained".

Singapore:

- A data centre moratorium was imposed in 2019 (datacentres.com, 29 April 2026).
- The second Data Centre Call for Application (DC-CFA2) was launched on 1 December 2025 and offered 200 MW. The Economic Development Board and IMDA fact sheet dated 21 August 2026 provisionally allocated 50 MW each to Digital Realty, Equinix, Keppel Data Centres and ST Telemedia Global Data Centres, all on Jurong Island.
- The 120 MW figure from datacentres.com conflicts with the official 200 MW and is superseded. Do not use it.
- Conditions reported for the 2026 round include a PUE of 1.3 or below, 100% renewable energy certificates, liquid cooling for at least 60% of IT load and waste heat recovery. The EDB fact sheet (21 August 2026, read in full) says more than 20 proposals were received. It also confirms the Green Mark DC Platinum certification requirement and at least 50% green energy supply for each project.
- The 2022 pilot and its 60 MW figure are not confirmed. Drop them.

Malaysia (Johor):

- From 1 August 2025, data centers pay a higher water tariff of RM5.33 per cubic metre, the first time they have paid a higher rate. Domestic Tier 1 and 2 users are unaffected. About 40 to 50 projects were in the pipeline and 20 had been approved (The Star, 2 August 2025).
- In late November 2025, the Johor state housing committee stopped approving Tier 1 and Tier 2 data centers over water use. The 50 million litres a day figure is a press estimate (The Star, 26 November 2025). The Tier 3 and 4 industrial-zone restriction is not in the source. Drop it.
- On 10 March 2026, the Johor Menteri Besar said high water-demand data centers would not be approved (Malay Mail).
- Dropped: the federal no-moratorium statement, the 2026 federal curb on non-AI data centers, and the tariff figures (39.96 to 45.62 sen per kWh).

Netherlands:

- In February 2022, the cabinet imposed a nine-month interim pause on new hyperscale data center permits (over 10 hectares or 70 MW) while planning rules were developed (DutchNews, 17 February 2022).
- The Amsterdam and Haarlemmermeer permit suspension of June 2019 could not be confirmed. Drop it unless checked.

Chile:

- On 27 February 2024, the Santiago Environmental Court partially upheld the challenge to Google's Cerrillos data center approval. It ordered the water assessment to include climate change effects on the Central Santiago Aquifer (Pauta, 27 February 2024). Say "partially upheld", not "annulled".
- The current project status is not confirmed.

Thailand's 2024 FDI inflow by source (from a lecture slide, BOT data, preliminary): China 21%, Singapore 11.3%, EU 10.1%, Japan 9.7%, US 9.5%, Taiwan 5.5%, ASEAN excluding Singapore 6%, Hong Kong 3.3%, others 23.6%. Verify against the BOT release before citing. Use it for the dependency condition: no single source dominates, although China is the largest at 21%.

Working thesis (proposal for the talk): data-center FDI is likely to improve Thailand's current account and tax base in the short run. Its gains for local firms and workers are likely to be weak unless local content, skills and cost assignment hold. The lectures support this, because spillovers depend on absorptive capacity and linkages, and services show little spillover support.

Gaps the lectures raise that the fact sheet does not answer:

- Spillover evidence specific to data centers. None was found. State spillover claims as conditional, and use the manufacturing and services evidence from the lectures.
- Thai absorptive-capacity indicators (human capital, financial market depth, technology gap). Not collected. A source check is needed before any slide states Thailand's level.
- Thai wage and skill-premium effects of data centers. None found. Use the lectures' general wage evidence and label it as general.
- Home-country effects (L5). Thailand is a host, so L5 serves as background only, except the point that export-oriented foreign sectors reduce home-market competition, as in the Ireland case.
