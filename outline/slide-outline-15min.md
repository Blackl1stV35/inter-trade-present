## CH1 — Policy details (4.0 min)

### S1 · 1.0 min · Germany, iron and steel: what do EU ETS and CBAM change?
- **Message:** We ask what the two policies change in the carbon-cost comparison for German steel, and what the record shows so far — descriptive, no causal claim.
- **Visual:** Title card with the scoped question; roadmap ribbon CH1 → CH2 → CH3; the 11 variable chips greyed; three stat tiles with definitions printed: manufacturing 19.9% of German GVA in 2024 vs EU27 15.9% (19.4% in 2025, provisional) [U22]; Germany 26% of EU sold industrial production, 2025 (a production share, not GDP) [U26]; German crude steel 34.1 Mt, 2025 [Dataset A, S13].
- **Say:** Why steel: first-stage input to automotive and machinery and a CBAM-covered sector [S4]. CBAM is EU law; Germany applies it [S1][S2]. Method: graphs, course theory, dated company cases; no regression.
- **Label:** Observed data · **Tags:** U22, U26, S4, S13

### S2 · 1.0 min · Why price carbon: emissions are a cost society bears *(needs lecturer OK)*
- **Message:** Steelmaking emissions cost society more than the producer pays; a carbon price makes producers face that cost — the logic of the ETS.
- **Visual:** Fig. 1 (`sources/images/base-doc-fig-01.png`, unchanged, SIMULATION footer kept). Beside it a before / during / latest strip: BEFORE — manufacturing production index 71.3 → 85.7 over 1995–2004 (2021 = 100) [U23], ETS Directive 2003; DURING — 2005 launch, pilot 2005–07 mostly free allocation, not a uniform charge [U1]; LATEST — covered power and industry emissions about 50% below 2005 in 2024, EU aggregate with the power sector an important driver, not Germany-steel, not CBAM [U29]. Caption: short-run static, one point in time.
- **Fallback if the graph is rejected:** two-line wedge statement plus the same strip.
- **Label:** Theoretical-illustrative · **Tags:** G1, G4, U1, U23, U29

### S3 · 1.0 min · Two tools, one logic: ETS inside, CBAM at the border
- **Message:** ETS prices emissions inside the EU; CBAM applies a comparable carbon-cost logic to covered imports by embedded emissions — not a ban, not a flat tariff.
- **Visual:** Two-column table, five rows (What / **Purpose** / Who surrenders / Price / Start). Purpose: ETS — cap and price emissions; CBAM — prevent carbon leakage (production or emissions shifting abroad). Start: 2005 pilot, mostly free allocation | reporting Oct 2023, definitive 1 Jan 2026. Footer chip: cleaner supplier → smaller bill (assumed example in A3).
- **Say:** Free allocation lowers net cost but is not an exemption [U1][U2]; certificate prices follow ETS auctions [U4][U5]; Germany sets no tariff of its own [S1][S2]. Coverage and the 50-tonne threshold → A4.
- **Label:** Policy rule · **Tags:** U1, U2, U4, U5, S1, S2

### S4 · 1.0 min · Timeline: ETS since 2005, CBAM payments from 2027
- **Message:** Carbon pricing arrived in stages and CBAM's cash payments start in 2027, so our output and trade data (latest 2025; steel trade balance 2024) contain no paid CBAM charge.
- **Visual:** Two-lane timeline. Lane 1 climate policy: 2005 ETS launch (phases 2008–12, 2013–20, 2021+) [U1]; 14 Jul 2021 CBAM proposed [S3]; 1 Oct 2023 reporting, no payments [U3][S7]; 1 Jan 2026 definitive [S6]; 1 Feb 2027 certificate sales, 30 Sep 2027 first surrender [U35][S5]; 2034 phase-in complete [S9]. Lane 2 separate trade measure, not CBAM: steel quota framework adopted 8 Jun 2026, applied 1 Jul 2026, 47% below 2024 safeguard quotas [U6][U8]; sanctions noted [U33]. Small step chart of the CBAM factor 97.5 → 0% over 2026–2034 [S9][S10]. Data-window bar: manufacturing 1995–2025, extra-EU imports 1999–2025, steel trade 2017–2024.
- **Label:** Policy rule · **Tags:** U1, U3, U6, U8, U35, U33, S3, S5–S7, S9, S10

## CH2 — Economic effects (8.5 min)

### S5 · 1.0 min · Theory: a carbon cost on imports, but not one-for-one *(needs lecturer OK)*
- **Message:** A carbon-related import cost narrows the import price advantage, but pass-through need not be full and domestic costs also changed, so output and imports respond in an open, short-run way.
- **Visual:** Redrawn Fig. 2 (the original full-pass-through figure is not shown): qualitative axes, no numbers, footer ILLUSTRATIVE; demand D; domestic supply S and a higher S′; import-price line rising by less than the full carbon cost; shift vs movement marked differently; labels — price paid up, quantity demanded down, domestic output ambiguous, import gap not guaranteed to fall. Strip with real facts:
  - BEFORE: industrial electricity (companies without reductions) 5.92 ct/kWh 2020 → 35.70 in 2022 [BaseNotes-3, SMARD, verify at source]; extra-EU imports already falling 3,337 → 2,697 kt in 2021–22 [U24].
  - DURING (reporting Oct 2023–Dec 2025, no payments): imports 2,089 → 1,464 kt, 2023 → 2025 [U24]; electricity 16.77 in 2024. Decline began in 2022 and annual data cannot isolate Q4 2023 onward.
  - AFTER (2026+): certificate prices EUR 75.36 / 75.28 / 82.32 per tCO2e, Q1–Q3 2026 [Dataset E]; no 2026 trade data, first payments 2027, no after-effect claim [U35].
- **Say up front (answers the CH2 brief):** because no CBAM payment exists in our data, CH2 gives theory predictions, descriptive context and a watch-list from February 2027 — not an estimated effect.
- **Label:** Theoretical-illustrative · **Chips lit:** M, Q, P · **Tags:** G1, G5, U3, U24, U35, Dataset E, BaseNotes-3
- **Fallback:** mechanism box with the same strip.

### S6 · 1.0 min · German manufacturing, 1995–2025
- **Message:** Real manufacturing output grew through the ETS launch, fell sharply in 2009 and 2020 and has softened since 2022; a graph alone cannot separate policy from energy, demand and COVID.
- **Visual:** Two stacked line panels, shared x-axis, separate y-axes: real GVA (2020 = 100) [U22] and production index (2021 = 100) [U23]; dashed markers at 2005 and Oct 2023; note "2026 definitive regime (beyond data)"; light bands 2009 and 2020. Tile: manufacturing share of total GVA 22.3% (1995) → 19.4% (2025); EU27 15.9% (2024) — value added, not GDP. Footnote 2023–25 GVA provisional. Footer: does not show an ETS or CBAM effect, or no effect → A1.
- **Label:** Observed data · **Chip lit:** Q only · **Tags:** U22, U23

### S7 · 1.5 min · Direct extra-EU steel imports, 1999–2025
- **Message:** Direct extra-EU Chapter 72 imports rose to 6,291 kt in 2007, were 3,342 kt in 2019 and have fallen each year after 2021 to 1,464 kt in 2025; overlapping events prevent reading this as a policy effect.
- **Visual:** Two stacked panels, 1999–2025: net mass (kt) and import value (EUR million, nominal) [U24]; text labels 2005 ETS launch, 2008–09 crisis, 2020 COVID, 2022 energy and war shock, Oct 2023 reporting. Caption: unit value EUR/t 736 (2019), 1,176 (2022), 1,087 (2025) — product-mix indicator, not a price index [Dataset C]. Scope badge: partner EXT_EU27_2020; Chapter 72 incl. scrap, excl. Chapter 73 and ore; not the CBAM base.
- **Say:** Imports rose after the 2005 launch to the 2007 peak, so we do not claim ETS cut imports. Boundary: steel cleared in another EU state is not counted — 1,955 kt direct extra-EU in 2024 versus 18.8 Mt all-partner steel-mill imports (different universes, roughly a tenth).
- **Label:** Observed data · **Chips lit:** M, Q, P · **Tags:** U24, U25, U1, U33, Dataset B, Dataset C

### S8 · 1.5 min · Net steel trade in tonnes: what we can and cannot show
- **Message:** On an all-partner physical basis the steel balance moved from −1.2 Mt (2017) to +4.1 Mt (2024) while imports fell faster than exports; the matched extra-EU export series is not in hand, so we draw no competitiveness verdict.
- **Visual:** One chart: exports 27.3 → 22.9 Mt and imports 28.5 → 18.8 Mt, 2017–2024, steel mill products, all partners incl. intra-EU, rounded [Dataset B]. Strip under the axis: approximate balance X − M (Mt): −1.2, −1.6, +0.3, +1.4, −0.4, +0.4, +3.0, +4.1 (derived from rounded values, about ±0.1). Small tile on its own axis: crude steel output 43.3 → 37.2 Mt (2017–24) — a production measure, not demand [Dataset A]. One-line gap box: not shown — matched extra-EU Chapter 72 exports (Comext flow 2, 1999–2025). Badge: not the CBAM base, not national-accounts NX (goods and services, EUR).
- **Say:** Member B's point — imports alone say nothing about competitiveness. NX = X − M; here steel only, tonnes. Never subtract this series from slide 7. Goods trade is not the balance of payments.
- **If the matched series is pulled before the freeze:** add it as a second panel (tonnes and EUR separate), update S10 and S12; keep wording neutral.
- **Label:** Observed data · **Chips lit:** X, M, NX (BOP footnote) · **Tags:** Dataset A, Dataset B, S12, S13, S18

### S9 · 1.0 min · What firms signed: plans, not yet delivered tonnes
- **Message:** Buyer and producer agreements show demand for lower-emission steel and announced plans, but they are plans and contracts, not delivered tonnes, cost parity or a CBAM effect.
- **Visual:** Three case rows with status chip, one green Opportunity line and one amber Limit line.
  1. Thyssenkrupp Steel × Volkswagen — MoU 22 Oct 2024, DRI plant commissioning from 2027 (gas first, then hydrogen), supply from 2028 [U10]; limit: future-supply MoU, not delivery; bluemint is a product family [U11].
  2. Salzgitter SALCOS × EWE — first DRP/EAF production 2027, full conversion mid-2030s [U12]; EWE green hydrogen contract 9 Jun 2026, 10,000 t/yr, 7 years [U13]; limit: about 6.5% of SALCOS hydrogen need; new energy inputs currently dearer than coal [U12][U13].
  3. Stegra (producer) with Mercedes-Benz (buyer, Jun 2023, about 50,000 t/yr) [U14] and BMW (buyer, 20 Oct 2021) [U15]; limit: 30 Sep 2026 review says more capital needed, 2025 delivery expectations are not verified deliveries [U16].
  Footer: all four agreements pre-date any CBAM payment and two pre-date reporting — direction, not cause; ETS, expected policy, subsidies (up to about EUR 2 bn for tkH2Steel, EUR 1 bn for SALCOS — A11), buyer targets and technology also matter. Does not show: delivered tonnes, cost parity → A13. Volvo–SSAB and Outokumpu are backup only.
- **Label:** Company announcement · **Chip lit:** I · **Tags:** U10–U16

## CH3 — Conclusion and suggestion (2.5 min)

### S12 · 1.0 min · Conclusion: can strengthen, if conditions hold
- **Message:** The record is consistent with ETS and CBAM supporting a move to cleaner steel, but a measured net German gain, cost parity and delivered tonnes are not shown.
- **Visual:** Verdict banner: EU ETS + CBAM can strengthen the long-term move to cleaner steel; the net German benefit is unmeasured and conditional. Three columns with per-item chips. CONSISTENT WITH: carbon-cost comparison now includes embedded emissions [U3] (Policy rule); dated commitments 2021–26 [U10][U12][U13][U14][U15][U16] (Company announcement); covered ETS emissions about 50% below 2005 in 2024, EU aggregate, power sector an important driver [U29] (Observed); OECD ≈10% emissions cut 2005–12 with no measured harm to regulated firms, earlier period [U30] (Published research). NOT SHOWN: causal size; net German GDP or welfare gain (scenario studies: smaller than sectoral effects and assumption-sensitive [S19]); cheaper green steel; delivered tonnes; paid-CBAM effects; extra-EU steel NX; **a measured outcome such as EAF or route share over time**. CONDITIONAL ON: affordable clean power and hydrogen, finance, verified emissions data, workable administration [U12][U20].
- **Label:** Mixed, per-item chips; verdict = Team judgement · **Tags:** U3, U10–U16, U20, U29, U30, S19

### S13 · 1.5 min · Suggestion: keep the framework, change the delivery
- **Message:** Keep a predictable ETS plus CBAM framework, improve how it is delivered, and reconsider only if monitoring shows leakage, losses that exceed benefits, or no verified fall in emissions per tonne.
- **Visual:** Three columns KEEP | CHANGE | RECONSIDER IF. Addressed to: EU legislators and the Commission (rules), the German government and DEHSt (support, monitoring, retraining).
  - KEEP: predictable ETS + CBAM with the legislated phase-in to 2034 [S9]; climate design separate from trade defence such as the steel quota [U6]; proportionate treatment of small importers — the 50-tonne threshold exempts 0.87% of embedded emissions, so coverage looks preserved [S23].
  - CHANGE: (1) emissions data, verification, transparent defaults [S11]; (2) clean power, hydrogen and scrap infrastructure with evaluated support; (3) monitor downstream users before any extension — still a proposal, Parliament amendments of 15 Sep 2026 referred to negotiation [U32][S24]; (4) retraining and regional adjustment [U9].
  - RECONSIDER IF: monitoring shows leakage or resource shuffling; downstream and regional losses exceed benefits; no verified fall in emissions per tonne.
  - Monitor strip: physical trade | input prices | domestic value added | realised investment | verified emissions per tonne | administration cost. Next data points: Feb 2027 sales, 30 Sep 2027 surrender.
- **Label:** Team judgement · **Tags:** S9, S11, S23, S24, U6, U9, U32
