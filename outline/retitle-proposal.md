## 2. The topic as submitted (4 October 2026)

- Title: *The EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM): Import Costs and Economic Trade-offs in Germany*
- Policy area: Environmental Protection Policy. Country: Germany.
- Focus: iron and steel imported from outside the EU.

## 3. Proposed title

**How EU ETS and CBAM Can Strengthen Europe's Long-Term Green Manufacturing Economy: The Case of Germany**

On-slide subtitle: *Germany, iron and steel; descriptive evidence.*

How we read "can strengthen": as a conditional statement. The policies are EU-wide, which is why the title says Europe, but the evidence is German. The conclusion slide says what the record is consistent with and what it does not show (causation, a net German gain, cost parity, delivered tonnes and a fall in CO2 per tonne are not shown).

## 4. Why we propose the change

1. **One system, not one tool.** CBAM is the import-side complement of the EU Emissions Trading System (ETS). The border charge only makes sense next to the domestic cap-and-trade price, so the report explains both.
2. **Timing of the data.** CBAM reporting began on 1 October 2023 and ran to the end of 2025. The definitive regime began on 1 January 2026, certificate sales start on 1 February 2027 and the first surrender is due on 30 September 2027. No payment has been made yet, so no data point can show the effect of a CBAM import cost on the German economy (2026 data are provisional context only). The ETS has been running since 2005 and gives a longer record.
3. **What the data showed.** After collecting trade, production and company data, we found that the import channel alone is small and sensitive to how imports are counted. Direct imports from outside the EU were 6.1% of German iron-and-steel import tonnes in 2025 (8.1% in 2024). In tonnes (HS Chapter 72, including scrap), imports from outside the EU cleared at German customs fell by 56% between 2019 and 2025, while imports counted by country of origin fell by 22% (in value, by 35% and 9%). A descriptive report that sets out opportunities and adjustment costs, and says clearly what is not shown, is more defensible than a claim about import costs.
4. **Fit with the course.** The report follows the course structure: a negative production externality and an import market with partial pass-through of a carbon cost on the main slides, spare diagrams on offshoring and subsidy in the backup, and the eleven variables you listed (table below).

## 5. What stays the same, what widens, and the topics

Unchanged: Environmental Protection Policy; Germany; iron and steel feeding manufacturing; a descriptive method based on course theory and published data, with no regression and no causal claim.

Widened: the policy changes from CBAM alone to the ETS–CBAM pair, and the trade focus changes from imports from outside the EU to also cover steel exports, net exports and company cases. The original import-cost question becomes one block inside CH2. The data window is long where the data allow it (manufacturing 1995–2025, ETS since 2005).
