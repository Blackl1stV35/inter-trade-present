## 3. Proposed title

On-slide subtitle: *Germany, iron and steel; descriptive evidence.*

## 4. Why we propose the change

1. **One system, not one tool.** CBAM is the import-side complement of the EU Emissions Trading System (ETS). The border charge only makes sense next to the domestic cap-and-trade price, so the report explains both.
2. **Timing of the data.** CBAM was in a reporting phase from 1 October 2023. The definitive regime began on 1 January 2026, certificate sales start on 1 February 2027 and the first surrender is due on 30 September 2027. No paid CBAM charge exists in the data yet, so an effect of CBAM import costs on the German economy cannot be shown. The ETS has been running since 2005 and gives a longer record.
3. **What the data showed.** After collecting the trade, production and company data, the import-cost reading turned out to depend on how imports are counted: steel cleared at German customs fell by 56% between 2019 and 2025, but steel counted by country of origin fell by 22%. A descriptive report that sets out opportunities and adjustment costs, and says clearly what is not shown, is more defensible than a claim about import costs.
4. **Fit with the course.** The report still follows the course structure and theory: negative production externality, an import market with partial pass-through of a carbon cost, sourcing and investment, and the variables you listed.

## 5. What stays the same and what the topics are

Unchanged: Environmental Protection Policy; Germany; iron and steel feeding manufacturing; a descriptive method based on course theory and published data, with no regression and no causal claim.
