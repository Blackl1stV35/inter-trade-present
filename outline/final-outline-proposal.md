## 3. CH1 — Policy details (4.0 min)

### S1 · 1.0 min · Germany, iron and steel: what do EU ETS and CBAM change?

- **Message:** We ask what the two policies change in the carbon-cost comparison for German steel and what the record shows so far; descriptive, no causal claim.
- **On the slide:** question and roadmap; three tiles with definitions printed. Manufacturing is 19.9% of German value added in 2024 (provisional) against 15.9% for the EU27; 19.4% in 2025 (provisional) [U22]. Germany holds 26% of EU sold industrial production, 2025, a production share and not GDP [U26]. German crude steel 34.3 Mt in 2025 (WV Stahl revision of Aug 2026; first estimate 34.1 Mt) [Dataset A, WV Stahl].
- **Say:** steel is the first-stage input to automotive and machinery and a CBAM-covered sector [S4]; CBAM is EU law and Germany applies it [S1][S2]; method is graphs, course theory and dated company cases, with no regression.
- **Limits:** the EU27 figure for 2025 is not published yet (Bulgaria and Latvia missing), so the comparison stays at 2024.
- **Label:** Observed data.

### S2 · 1.0 min · Why price carbon: emissions are a cost society bears *(lecturer check pending)*

- **Message:** Steelmaking emissions cost society more than the producer pays; a carbon price makes producers face that cost, which is the logic of the ETS.
- **On the slide:** Fig. 1 below, unchanged, with its SIMULATION footer. Beside it a before / during / latest strip. BEFORE: manufacturing production index rose from 71.3 to 85.7 over 1995–2004 (2021 = 100) [U23]. DURING: 2005 launch; the pilot 2005–07 used mostly free allocation and was not a uniform charge [U1]. LATEST: covered EU power and industry emissions were about 50% below 2005 in 2024, an EU aggregate in which the power sector was an important driver; not German steel and not CBAM [U29].
- **Fallback:** a two-line wedge statement plus the same strip.
- **Label:** Theoretical-illustrative.

![Fig. 1: negative production externality (invented values)](sources/images/base-doc-fig-01.png)

### S3 · 1.0 min · Two tools, one logic: ETS inside, CBAM at the border

- **Message:** ETS prices emissions inside the EU; CBAM applies a comparable carbon-cost logic to covered imports by embedded emissions; it is neither a ban nor a flat tariff.
- **On the slide:** five-row table, cells of about eight words. What: declining cap, tradable allowances | carbon cost on covered imports by embedded emissions. **Purpose:** cap and price emissions | prevent carbon leakage (production or emissions shifting abroad). Who surrenders: installations surrender allowances | authorised declarant surrenders certificates. Price: auction price | average of EU ETS auction prices, quarterly in 2026, weekly from 2027. Start: 2005 pilot, mostly free allocation | reporting Oct 2023, definitive 1 Jan 2026.
- **Say:** free allocation lowers net cost but is not an exemption [U1][U2]; Germany sets no tariff of its own [S1][S2]. Coverage and the 50-tonne threshold are in A4; the worked example is in A3.
- **Label:** Policy rule.

### S4 · 1.0 min · Timeline: ETS since 2005, CBAM payments from 2027

- **Message:** Carbon pricing arrived in stages and CBAM's cash payments start in 2027; our annual data end in 2025 and monthly data run to August 2026, so no data point includes a paid CBAM charge.
- **On the slide:** two-lane timeline. Lane 1 climate policy: 2005 ETS launch [U1]; 14 Jul 2021 CBAM proposed [S3]; 1 Oct 2023 reporting [U3][S7]; 1 Jan 2026 definitive regime [S6]; 1 Feb 2027 certificate sales and 30 Sep 2027 first surrender [U35][S5]; 2034 end of the phase-in under current law. Small step chart of the CBAM factor, 97.5% (2026) to 0% (2034) [S9][S10], with a footnote: a Commission proposal of 17 Jul 2026 would keep 15% for 2034–37 and end at 2038; not law, Council debated it on 12 Oct without a decision. Lane 2, a separate trade measure that is not CBAM: Regulation (EU) 2026/1384, Council adoption 8 Jun 2026, applies 1 Jul 2026; duty-free quota 18.3 Mt a year, about 47% below the 2024 safeguard quotas; out-of-quota duty 50% (the safeguard's was 25%). Data-window bar: manufacturing 1995–2025; extra-EU Chapter 72 trade 1999–2025 annual plus Jan–Aug 2026 monthly (provisional); steel trade all partners 2017–2024.
- **Label:** Policy rule.

## 4. CH2 — Economic effects (8.5 min)

### S5 · 1.0 min · Theory: a carbon cost on imports, but not one-for-one *(lecturer check pending)*

- **Message:** A carbon-related import cost narrows the import price advantage, but pass-through need not be full and domestic costs also changed, so output and imports respond in an open, short-run way.
- **On the slide:** the redrawn Fig. 2 below (qualitative, no numbers, ILLUSTRATIVE banner). Beneath it a before / during / after strip with real facts:
  - BEFORE: industrial electricity for companies without reductions rose from 5.92 ct/kWh (2020) to 35.70 (2022) [BaseNotes-3, SMARD; see section 6]; extra-EU Chapter 72 imports were already falling, 3,337 kt (2021) to 2,697 kt (2022) [Comext].
  - DURING (reporting Oct 2023–Dec 2025, no payments): imports 2,089 kt (2023) to 1,464 kt (2025) [Comext]; electricity 16.77 ct/kWh in 2024.
  - AFTER (2026): Jan–Aug 2026 imports 1,108 kt against 1,028 kt in Jan–Aug 2025, +7.7% in tonnes and −0.7% in value (provisional) [Comext]; CBAM certificate prices EUR 75.36, 75.28 and 82.32 per tCO2e for Q1–Q3 2026, Q4 due 4 Jan 2027 [Dataset E]; first payments 2027; no after-effect claim.
- **Say up front (answers the CH2 brief):** because no CBAM payment exists in our data, CH2 gives theory predictions, descriptive context and a watch-list from February 2027, not an estimated effect. "Our talk falls inside Q4, so the Q4 price does not exist yet."
- **Fallback:** a mechanism box with the same strip.
- **Label:** Theoretical-illustrative. **Chips lit:** M, Q, P.

### S7 · 1.5 min · Direct extra-EU steel imports, 1999–2025

- **Message:** Direct extra-EU Chapter 72 imports rose to 6,291 kt in 2007, were 3,342 kt in 2019 and have fallen each year after 2021 to 1,464 kt in 2025; overlapping events prevent reading this as a policy effect.
- **On the slide:** the chart below (tonnes and euros on separate panels); caption: unit value EUR/t 736 (2019), 1,176 (2022), 1,087 (2025), a product-mix indicator and not a price index [Dataset C]. Small separate inset, labelled provisional and same months: Jan–Aug 2025 against Jan–Aug 2026, 1,028 to 1,108 kt (+7.7%), EUR 1,127 m to 1,119 m (−0.7%).
- **Say:** imports rose after the 2005 launch to the 2007 peak, so we do not claim ETS cut imports. Materiality: this extra-EU series is 6.1% of German Chapter 72 import tonnes in 2025 (8.1% in 2024, 13.4% in 2017) and 16% of export tonnes; about 88% of German two-way Chapter 72 tonnage is with other EU states. Heading 7203 (direct-reduced iron) fell from 1,016 kt in 2017 to 28 kt in 2025 and accounts for 31% of the 2017–25 fall; the cause is not established. Do not quote single months (monthly year-on-year swings run from −44% to +49%).
- **Limits:** direct trade only; non-EU steel cleared in another EU state counts as intra-EU. Not the CBAM base. 2023 was revised slightly in the 16 Oct 2026 vintage (rounds the same).
- **Label:** Observed data. **Chips lit:** M, Q, P. **Tags:** Comext DS-045409, U24, U25, Dataset C.

### S8 · 1.5 min · Steel trade balance: what the matched extra-EU series shows

- **Message:** The extra-EU Chapter 72 balance was positive in every year since 1999 (+2.6 Mt in 2017, +3.2 Mt in 2025) and widened because imports fell faster than exports; the all-partner steel balance also turned positive; none of this is a competitiveness verdict.
- **On the slide:** the two-panel chart below (two universes, never subtracted from each other). Left: all partners incl. intra-EU, steel mill products, 2017–2024, exports 27.3 to 22.9 Mt and imports 28.5 to 18.8 Mt, balance −1.2 to +4.1 Mt (derived from rounded values) [WV Stahl, Dataset B]. Right: direct extra-EU Chapter 72, 2017–2025, exports 7,217 to 4,681 kt, imports 4,624 to 1,464 kt, balance +2.6 to +3.2 Mt [Comext]. One-line tile: Jan–Aug 2026 (provisional), exports −16.3%, imports +7.7%, net balance −26.9% in tonnes; causes not tested.
- **Say:** Member B's point: imports alone say nothing about competitiveness, so we add exports. NX is X minus M; here it is steel only, in tonnes. From 2017 to 2025 extra-EU imports fell 68% and exports 35%, and 2025 exports are the lowest in 27 years; the surplus widened because imports fell faster. In 2017 the extra-EU balance was +2.6 Mt, the intra-EU balance −3.5 Mt and the all-partner balance −0.9 Mt, so never mix series. Goods trade is not the balance of payments.
- **Limits:** Chapter 72 includes scrap, pig iron and DRI; steel mill products do not; levels differ and the sign differs in 2021. Not national-accounts NX (goods and services, EUR). Not the CBAM base.
- **Label:** Observed data. **Chips lit:** X, M, NX (BOP footnote). **Tags:** Comext, Dataset B, S12, S18.

## 5. CH3 — Conclusion and suggestion (2.5 min)

### S12 · 1.0 min · Conclusion: can strengthen, if conditions hold

- **Message:** The record is consistent with ETS and CBAM supporting a move to cleaner steel, but a measured net German gain, cost parity, delivered tonnes and a fall in CO2 per tonne are not shown.
- **On the slide:** verdict banner: EU ETS + CBAM can strengthen the long-term move to cleaner steel; the net German benefit is unmeasured and conditional. Three columns.
  - CONSISTENT WITH: the carbon-cost comparison now includes embedded emissions [U3] (Policy rule); dated commitments 2021–26 [U10][U12][U13][U14][U15] (Company announcement); extra-EU steel surplus in every year, widening as imports fell faster (Observed, not an effect estimate); covered EU ETS emissions about 50% below 2005 in 2024, an EU aggregate with the power sector an important driver [U29] (Observed); OECD: about 10% emissions cut in 2005–12 with no measured harm to regulated firms, earlier period [U30] (Published research).
  - NOT SHOWN: a fall in CO2 per tonne (electric-arc share 29.2% in 1999 and 30.5% in 2025; ETS-reported CO2 per tonne of crude steel 0.84 in 2013 and 0.88 in 2025; WV Stahl specific CO2 1.37 in 2019 and 1.39 in 2025); causal size; net German GDP or welfare gain (scenario studies: smaller than sectoral effects, assumption-sensitive [S19]); cheaper green steel; delivered tonnes; tax and net budget effects; paid-CBAM effects (first payments 2027).
  - CONDITIONAL ON: affordable clean power and hydrogen; finance; new low-carbon plants running (first output 2027 or later); verified emissions data; workable administration [U12][U20].
- **Say:** German ETS-reported emissions of iron and steel installations fell about 16% between 2019 and 2025 while crude steel fell about 14%, so the fall follows output; per tonne nothing has moved yet.
- **Label:** mixed, per-item chips; verdict = Team judgement.

### S13 · 1.5 min · Suggestion: keep the framework, change the delivery

- **Message:** Keep a predictable ETS plus CBAM framework, improve how it is delivered, and reconsider only if monitoring shows leakage, losses that exceed benefits, or no verified fall in emissions per tonne.
- **Addressed to:** EU legislators and the Commission (rules); the German government and the emissions-trading authority DEHSt (support, monitoring, retraining).
- **On the slide:** three columns.
  - KEEP: ETS + CBAM with the phase-in to 2034 under current law (a 17 Jul 2026 proposal would stretch it to 2038; undecided) [S9]; climate design kept apart from trade defence such as the steel quota [U6]; proportionate treatment of small importers, since the 50-tonne threshold would exempt 0.87% of embedded emissions (Commission assessment of 30 Sep 2026, limit 1%) [S23].
  - CHANGE: (1) emissions data, verification and transparent defaults [S11]; (2) clean power, hydrogen and scrap infrastructure with evaluated support; (3) monitor downstream users before any extension, which is a proposal only: Commission 17 Dec 2025 (about 180 products, from 1 Jan 2028), Council position 12 Jun 2026, Parliament position 15 Sep 2026 (464–50–159), negotiations under way, not law [U32][S24]; (4) retraining and regional adjustment [U9]; (5) keep carbon receipts working for the transition: ETS receipts go to the climate fund by law, the 2027 draft budget would divert EUR 2.7 bn to the core budget (Bundestag vote planned 27 Nov 2026), and CBAM receipts have no earmark.
  - RECONSIDER IF: monitoring shows leakage or resource shuffling; downstream and regional losses exceed benefits; no verified fall in emissions per tonne from the 2025 baseline (0.88 t CO2 per tonne of crude steel, ETS-reported; 1.39 t including purchased power, WV Stahl).
  - Monitor strip: physical trade | input prices | domestic value added | realised investment | verified emissions per tonne | use of carbon receipts. Next data points: Eurostat update 13 Nov, Bundestag vote 27 Nov 2026, certificate sales 1 Feb 2027, surrender 30 Sep 2027.
- **Label:** Team judgement.
