### Corrections to the research workbook found in this pass

These change what the deck may say; each is already applied below.

1. **Electricity (slide 5, backup A10).** The workbook's 5.92 ct/kWh for 2020 is the SMARD price *with* all possible reductions, not without. The no-reductions price was about 17.1 ct/kWh in 2020, so "nearly triple the 2020 level" is wrong: the no-reductions price in 2024 (16.1 in the 22 Oct 2026 SMARD vintage, 16.8 in the Feb 2025 vintage) is slightly below 2020. The "+77%" figure is a with-reductions comparison and exists only in the Feb 2025 vintage (+65% in the current vintage). Invoiced prices for the largest consumers (150,000 MWh a year and more) did rise, from 6.88 to 13.01 ct/kWh in 2020–24 (+89%).
2. **Direct versus by-origin imports (slide 7).** Direct extra-EU Chapter 72 imports (cleared at German customs) were only 37% of German non-EU-origin imports in 2025 (66% in 2019), mostly a change since 2022 whose cause is not identified. The direct series fell 56% (2019–25); non-EU steel counted by origin, including what arrives via other EU states, fell 22%.
3. **Steel trade after 2024 (slide 8).** WV Stahl's 2026 edition revises 2024 and adds 2025: the all-partner balance narrowed from +4.2 Mt (2024) to +1.9 Mt (2025) as imports rose (+7.9%) and exports fell (−3.9%). Since 2017 imports are still down more than exports (−28% against −18%).
4. **Public support (slides 9, 11, backup A11, A13).** Salzgitter's aid is about EUR 1.3 bn since February 2026 (EUR 1.076 bn paid by 30 Jun 2026), not EUR 1 bn; Thyssenkrupp's "full hydrogen by 2029" was a 2023/2024 company target and is stale; the carbon-contract thresholds are now 50% within four years and 85% at the end, not 60% / 90%; EUR 6.9 bn was a July 2024 commitment total (about EUR 5.9 bn live); EUR 12.5 bn total cost and 11.5 Mt a year could not be verified.

## 3. CH1 — Policy details (4.0 min)

### S1 · 1.0 min · Germany, iron and steel: what does the record show since EU ETS and CBAM?

- **Message:** We ask what the record shows for German steel since EU ETS and CBAM, and which opportunities and adjustment costs appear; descriptive, no causal claim.
- **On the slide:** question and roadmap; three tiles with definitions printed. Manufacturing is 19.9% of German value added in 2024 (provisional) against 15.9% for the EU27; 19.4% in 2025 (provisional) [U22]. Germany holds 26% of EU sold industrial production, 2025, a production share and not GDP [U26]. German crude steel 34.1 Mt in 2025 (WV Stahl / Destatis, 2026 edition; revised to 34.3 Mt in Aug 2026) [Dataset A].
- **Say:** steel is the first-stage input to automotive and machinery and a CBAM-covered sector [S4]; CBAM is EU law and Germany applies it [S1][S2]; method is graphs, course theory and dated company cases, with no regression.
- **Limits:** the EU27 figure for 2025 is not published yet (Bulgaria and Latvia missing), so the comparison stays at 2024.
- **Label:** Observed data.

### S2 · 1.0 min · Why price carbon: emissions are a cost society bears *(lecturer check pending)*

- **Message:** Steelmaking emissions cost society more than the producer pays; a carbon price makes producers face that cost at the margin, which is the logic of the ETS.
- **On the slide:** Fig. 1 below, unchanged, with its SIMULATION footer. Beside it a before / during / latest strip. BEFORE: manufacturing production index rose from 71.3 to 85.7 over 1995–2004 (2021 = 100) [U23]. DURING: 2005 launch; the pilot 2005–07 used mostly free allocation and was not a uniform charge [U1]. LATEST: covered EU power and industry emissions were about 50% below 2005 in 2024, an EU aggregate led by the power sector; not German steel and not CBAM [U29].
- **Say:** the price is a marginal incentive; free allocation covered about 94% of German iron-and-steel emissions in 2024 (adjusted for process gases passed on), so the net bill was small and CBAM's phase-in is what changes the border comparison [DEHSt VET 2024].
- **Fallback:** a two-line wedge statement plus the same strip.
- **Label:** Theoretical-illustrative.

### S3 · 1.0 min · Two tools, one logic: ETS inside, CBAM at the border

- **Message:** ETS prices emissions inside the EU; CBAM applies a comparable carbon-cost logic to covered imports by embedded emissions; it is neither a ban nor a flat tariff.
- **On the slide:** five-row table, cells of about eight words. What: declining cap, tradable allowances | carbon cost on covered imports by embedded emissions. **Purpose:** cap and price emissions | prevent carbon leakage (production or emissions shifting abroad). Who surrenders: installations surrender allowances | authorised declarant surrenders certificates. Price: auction price | average of EU ETS auction prices, quarterly in 2026, weekly from 2027. Start: 2005 pilot, mostly free allocation | reporting Oct 2023, definitive 1 Jan 2026.
- **Say:** free allocation lowers net cost but is not an exemption [U1][U2]; German auction prices averaged EUR 14.9 a tonne in 2018, 83.7 in 2023 and 73.9 in 2025 [UBA / EEX]; Germany sets no tariff of its own [S1][S2]. Coverage and the 50-tonne threshold are in A4; the worked example is in A3.
- **Label:** Policy rule.

### S4 · 1.0 min · Timeline: ETS since 2005, CBAM payments from 2027

- **Message:** Carbon pricing arrived in stages and CBAM's cash payments start in 2027; our annual data end in 2025 and monthly trade data run to August 2026, so no data point includes a paid CBAM charge.
- **On the slide:** two-lane timeline. Lane 1 climate policy: 2005 ETS launch [U1]; 14 Jul 2021 CBAM proposed [S3]; 4 Oct 2022 and 20 Jul 2023 EU approval of German aid for Salzgitter and Thyssenkrupp; 1 Oct 2023 reporting [U3][S7]; 1 Jan 2026 definitive regime [S6]; 1 Feb 2027 certificate sales and 30 Sep 2027 first surrender [U35][S5] (the Q4 2026 price is due 4 Jan 2027); 2034 end of the phase-in under current law. Small step chart of the CBAM factor, 97.5% (2026) to 0% (2034) [S9][S10], with a footnote: a Commission proposal of 17 Jul 2026 would keep 15% for 2034–37 and end at 2038; not law, Council debated it on 12 Oct without a decision; the team supports the stretch (S13). Lane 2, a separate trade measure that is not CBAM: Regulation (EU) 2026/1384, Council adoption 8 Jun 2026, applies 1 Jul 2026; duty-free quota 18.3 Mt a year, about 47% below the 2024 safeguard quotas; out-of-quota duty 50% (the safeguard's was 25%).
- **Speaker note:** data windows are manufacturing 1995–2025; extra-EU Chapter 72 trade 1999–2025 annual plus Jan–Aug 2026 monthly (provisional); steel trade all partners 2017–2025; prices to September 2026.
- **Label:** Policy rule.

## 4. CH2 — Economic effects (8.5 min)

### S5 · 1.0 min · Theory: a carbon cost on imports, but not one-for-one *(lecturer check pending)*

- **Message:** A carbon-related import cost narrows the import price advantage, but pass-through need not be full and domestic costs may also change, so output and imports respond in an open, short-run way.
- **On the slide:** the redrawn Fig. 2 below (qualitative, no numbers, ILLUSTRATIVE banner). Beneath it a before / during / after strip with real facts, imports shown both ways (direct = cleared at German customs; by origin = all channels):
  - BEFORE: direct imports 4,624 kt (2017) to 2,697 kt (2022); by origin 6,933 to 4,704 kt [Comext, Destatis]. Modelled industrial electricity price without reductions 17.1 ct/kWh (2020) to 35.9 (2022) [SMARD, vintage of 22 Oct 2026].
  - DURING (reporting from 1 Oct 2023; 2023 straddles the start and is not assigned): direct imports 1,955 kt (2024) to 1,464 kt (2025); by origin 4,156 to 3,919 kt. Electricity 16.1 ct/kWh in 2024 and 16.5 in 2025.
  - AFTER (2026, provisional): Jan–Aug direct imports 1,108 kt against 1,028 kt in Jan–Aug 2025, +7.7% in tonnes and −0.7% in value [Comext]; by origin Jan–Jul +6.1% [Destatis]; electricity 17.3 ct/kWh for Jan–Sep 2026; CBAM certificate prices EUR 75.36, 75.28 and 82.32 per tCO2e for Q1–Q3 2026, Q4 due 4 Jan 2027 [Dataset E]; first payments 2027; no after-effect claim.
- **Say up front (answers the CH2 brief):** because no CBAM payment exists in our data, CH2 gives theory predictions, descriptive context and a watch-list from February 2027, not an estimated effect. "Our talk falls inside Q4, so the Q4 price does not exist yet." By origin, imports were flat inside the reporting window (3,799 kt in 2023, 3,919 in 2025), so part of the direct fall reflects where steel is cleared.
- **Limits:** SMARD is a Bundesnetzagentur model, not invoiced prices; it states no consumption band and is not steel-specific. Invoiced prices for the largest consumers rose 89% in 2020–24, so energy costs did rise for big users; never say "steelmakers paid" or "tripled". Net ETS cost is small under free allocation, so the supply shift in Fig. 2 is a possibility, not a finding.
- **Fallback:** a mechanism box with the same strip.
- **Label:** Theoretical-illustrative; the electricity strip is a Model-based result. **Chips lit:** M, Q, P.

### S11 · 1.0 min · Costs and conditions: what has to go right

- **Message:** Cleaner steel is still a premium or a gap in many settings, so any long-term benefit is conditional on clean power and hydrogen, finance, delivery and regional adjustment.
- **On the slide:** three cards and a banner. Price gap: IEA says hydrogen cost gaps often remain even with policy support [U20]; the IEA steel and cement report stresses buyer commitments and public policy [U19]; Salzgitter says new energy inputs currently cost more than coal [U12]. Capital and delivery: Duisburg has committed support of up to about EUR 2 bn (EUR 550 m grant plus up to EUR 1.45 bn conditional operating payment) against a company-stated plant cost of about EUR 3 bn (reported rising), and starts on natural gas; Stegra needs more capital to finish Boden, amount not published, no start date, new chief executive from 1 Nov 2026 [U16]; EWE covers about 6.5% of SALCOS hydrogen need [U13]. People and regions: Thyssenkrupp's plan of 25 Nov 2024 is about 5,000 job reductions by 2030 plus about 6,000 transferred or sold; the company cites weak competitiveness and cheap imports; it pre-dates definitive CBAM and is not attributed to the policy [U9]. Banner: we do not say green steel is cheaper.

## 5. CH3 — Conclusion and suggestion (2.5 min)

### S12 · 1.0 min · Conclusion: consistent with a move toward cleaner steel; causation not shown

- **Message:** The record is consistent with a move toward cleaner steel, but causation, a net German gain, cost parity, delivered tonnes and a fall in CO2 per tonne are not shown. The assigned title, "can strengthen", is a team judgement conditional on the list below.
- **On the slide:** verdict banner: the record is consistent with a move toward cleaner steel; ETS and CBAM causation and the net German benefit are not shown. Three columns.
  - CONSISTENT WITH: the carbon-cost comparison now includes embedded emissions [U3] (Policy rule); dated commitments 2021–26 [U10][U12][U13][U14][U15] (Company announcement; the flagship aid approvals pre-date reporting but not the 2021 proposal or the ETS price rise); OECD: about 10% emissions cut in 2005–12 with no measured harm to regulated firms, earlier period, sample not specified [U30] (Published research).
  - NOT SHOWN: a sustained fall in CO2 per tonne (electric-arc share 29.2% in 1999, 27.7–32% in 2020–25, 30.5% in 2025; ETS-reported emissions divided by crude steel, derived, 0.84 in 2013, 0.90 in 2019, 0.88 in 2025; WV Stahl specific CO2 1.37 in 2019 and 1.39 in 2025); causal size; net German GDP or welfare gain (scenario studies: smaller than sectoral effects, assumption-sensitive [S19]); cheaper green steel; delivered tonnes; tax and net budget effects; paid-CBAM effects (first payments 2027). Counter-evidence: ArcelorMittal withdrew (approved aid unused), SALCOS slipped to 2027, Stegra needs capital.
  - CONDITIONAL ON: affordable clean power and hydrogen; finance; new low-carbon plants running (first output 2027 or later); verified emissions data; workable administration [U12][U20].
  - Context line, no steel inference: covered EU ETS emissions about 50% below 2005 in 2024, an EU aggregate led by the power sector [U29].
- **Say:** German ETS-reported emissions of iron and steel installations fell about 16% between 2019 and 2025 while crude steel fell about 14%, so the fall largely follows output; per tonne there is no clear change (ETS-derived −2.5%, WV Stahl +1.5%, within noise).
- **Label:** Team judgement.

### S13 · 1.5 min · Suggestion: keep the framework, change the delivery

- **Message:** Keep a predictable ETS plus CBAM framework, improve how it is delivered, and reconsider only if monitoring shows leakage, losses that exceed benefits, or no sustained fall in emissions per tonne.
- **Addressed to:** EU legislators and the Commission (rules); the German government and the emissions-trading authority DEHSt (support, monitoring, retraining).
- **On the slide:** three columns. Continue verdict in one sentence: continue, with the changes below; nothing to drop now.
  - KEEP: ETS + CBAM, and support the Commission's proposal of 17 Jul 2026 to stretch the CBAM-sector phase-out (factor 15% in 2034–37, ending in 2038 instead of 2034; not yet law) [S9]. Reasons: the first low-carbon plants only start in 2027 or later, and free allocation still covered about 94% of German steel emissions in 2024, so a faster phase-out would remove protection before clean capacity exists. Trade-off: carbon costs align more slowly at the border, so the stretch is tied to the monitoring triggers below. Also keep climate design apart from trade defence such as the steel quota [U6]; proportionate treatment of small importers, since the 50-tonne threshold would exempt 0.87% of embedded emissions (Commission assessment of 30 Sep 2026, limit 1%) [S23].
  - CHANGE: (1) emissions data, verification and transparent defaults [S11]; (2) clean power, hydrogen and scrap infrastructure with evaluated support and workable conditions (the hydrogen requirement of the Duisburg aid was reported relaxed in Aug 2026); (3) monitor downstream users before any extension, which is a proposal only: Commission 17 Dec 2025 (about 180 products, from 1 Jan 2028), Council position 12 Jun 2026, Parliament position 15 Sep 2026 (464–50–159), negotiations under way, not law [U32][S24]; (4) retraining and regional adjustment [U9]; (5) keep carbon receipts working for the transition: ETS receipts go to the climate fund by law, the 2027 draft budget would divert EUR 2.7 bn to the core budget (Bundestag vote planned 27 Nov 2026), and CBAM receipts have no earmark.
  - DROP OR REVISE IF: monitoring shows leakage or resource shuffling; downstream and regional losses exceed benefits; no sustained fall in emissions per tonne; or the longer free-allocation period is not matched by new low-carbon capacity starting, which removes the reason for the stretch. The trigger is qualitative: baseline 2025 (0.88 t CO2 per tonne of crude steel, ETS-derived, direct emissions only; 1.39 t including purchased power, WV Stahl), compared at similar utilisation because 2025 utilisation was below 70%.
  - Monitor strip: physical trade | input prices | domestic value added | realised investment | verified emissions per tonne | use of carbon receipts. Next data points: Eurostat update 13 Nov, Bundestag vote 27 Nov 2026, certificate sales 1 Feb 2027, surrender 30 Sep 2027.
- **Label:** Team judgement.
