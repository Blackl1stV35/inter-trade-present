### Corrections to the research workbook found in this pass

These change what the deck may say; each is already applied below.

1. **Electricity (slide 5, backup A10).** The workbook's 5.92 ct/kWh for 2020 is the SMARD price *with* all possible reductions, not without. The no-reductions price was about 17.1 ct/kWh in 2020, so the claim "nearly triple the 2020 level" is wrong: the no-reductions price in 2024 (16.1–16.8, depending on the SMARD vintage) is slightly *below* 2020. The "+77%" figure is correct but is a with-reductions comparison.
2. **Direct versus by-origin imports (slide 7).** Direct extra-EU Chapter 72 imports (cleared at German customs) were only 37% of German non-EU-origin imports in 2025 (66% in 2019). The direct series fell 56% (2019–25); non-EU steel counted by origin, including what arrives via other EU states, fell 22%.
3. **Steel trade after 2024 (slide 8).** WV Stahl's 2026 edition revises 2024 and adds 2025: the all-partner balance narrowed from +4.2 Mt (2024) to +1.9 Mt (2025) because imports rose. "Imports fell faster than exports" holds only to 2024.
4. **Public support (slides 9, 11, backup A11, A13).** Salzgitter's aid is about EUR 1.3 bn since February 2026 (EUR 1.076 bn paid by 30 Jun 2026), not EUR 1 bn; Thyssenkrupp's "full hydrogen by 2029" was a 2024 company target and is stale; the carbon-contract thresholds are now 50% within four years and 85% at the end, not 60% / 90%; EUR 6.9 bn was a July 2024 commitment total (about EUR 5.9 bn live); EUR 12.5 bn total cost and 11.5 Mt a year could not be verified.

## 3. CH1 — Policy details (4.0 min)

### S1 · 1.0 min · Germany, iron and steel: what do EU ETS and CBAM change?

- **Message:** We ask what the two policies change in the carbon-cost comparison for German steel and what the record shows so far; descriptive, no causal claim.
- **On the slide:** question and roadmap; three tiles with definitions printed. Manufacturing is 19.9% of German value added in 2024 (provisional) against 15.9% for the EU27; 19.4% in 2025 (provisional) [U22]. Germany holds 26% of EU sold industrial production, 2025, a production share and not GDP [U26]. German crude steel 34.1 Mt in 2025 (WV Stahl / Destatis, 2026 edition; revised to 34.3 Mt in Aug 2026) [Dataset A].
- **Say:** steel is the first-stage input to automotive and machinery and a CBAM-covered sector [S4]; CBAM is EU law and Germany applies it [S1][S2]; method is graphs, course theory and dated company cases, with no regression.
- **Limits:** the EU27 figure for 2025 is not published yet (Bulgaria and Latvia missing), so the comparison stays at 2024.
- **Label:** Observed data.

### S2 · 1.0 min · Why price carbon: emissions are a cost society bears *(lecturer check pending)*

- **Message:** Steelmaking emissions cost society more than the producer pays; a carbon price makes producers face that cost, which is the logic of the ETS.
- **On the slide:** Fig. 1 below, unchanged, with its SIMULATION footer. Beside it a before / during / latest strip. BEFORE: manufacturing production index rose from 71.3 to 85.7 over 1995–2004 (2021 = 100) [U23]. DURING: 2005 launch; the pilot 2005–07 used mostly free allocation and was not a uniform charge [U1]. LATEST: covered EU power and industry emissions were about 50% below 2005 in 2024, an EU aggregate in which the power sector was an important driver; not German steel and not CBAM [U29].
- **Fallback:** a two-line wedge statement plus the same strip.
- **Label:** Theoretical-illustrative.

### S3 · 1.0 min · Two tools, one logic: ETS inside, CBAM at the border

- **Message:** ETS prices emissions inside the EU; CBAM applies a comparable carbon-cost logic to covered imports by embedded emissions; it is neither a ban nor a flat tariff.
- **On the slide:** five-row table, cells of about eight words. What: declining cap, tradable allowances | carbon cost on covered imports by embedded emissions. **Purpose:** cap and price emissions | prevent carbon leakage (production or emissions shifting abroad). Who surrenders: installations surrender allowances | authorised declarant surrenders certificates. Price: auction price | average of EU ETS auction prices, quarterly in 2026, weekly from 2027. Start: 2005 pilot, mostly free allocation | reporting Oct 2023, definitive 1 Jan 2026.
- **Say:** free allocation lowers net cost but is not an exemption [U1][U2]; Germany sets no tariff of its own [S1][S2]. Coverage and the 50-tonne threshold are in A4; the worked example is in A3.
- **Label:** Policy rule.

### S4 · 1.0 min · Timeline: ETS since 2005, CBAM payments from 2027

- **Message:** Carbon pricing arrived in stages and CBAM's cash payments start in 2027; our annual data end in 2025 and monthly data run to August 2026, so no data point includes a paid CBAM charge.
- **On the slide:** two-lane timeline. Lane 1 climate policy: 2005 ETS launch [U1]; 4 Oct 2022 and 20 Jul 2023 EU approval of German aid for Salzgitter and Thyssenkrupp (before CBAM reporting); 14 Jul 2021 CBAM proposed [S3]; 1 Oct 2023 reporting [U3][S7]; 1 Jan 2026 definitive regime [S6]; 1 Feb 2027 certificate sales and 30 Sep 2027 first surrender [U35][S5]; 2034 end of the phase-in under current law. Small step chart of the CBAM factor, 97.5% (2026) to 0% (2034) [S9][S10], with a footnote: a Commission proposal of 17 Jul 2026 would keep 15% for 2034–37 and end at 2038; not law, Council debated it on 12 Oct without a decision. Lane 2, a separate trade measure that is not CBAM: Regulation (EU) 2026/1384, Council adoption 8 Jun 2026, applies 1 Jul 2026; duty-free quota 18.3 Mt a year, about 47% below the 2024 safeguard quotas; out-of-quota duty 50% (the safeguard's was 25%). Data-window bar: manufacturing 1995–2025; extra-EU Chapter 72 trade 1999–2025 annual plus Jan–Aug 2026 monthly (provisional); steel trade all partners 2017–2025.
- **Label:** Policy rule.



## 4. CH2 — Economic effects (8.5 min)

### S5 · 1.0 min · Theory: a carbon cost on imports, but not one-for-one *(lecturer check pending)*

- **Message:** A carbon-related import cost narrows the import price advantage, but pass-through need not be full and domestic costs also changed, so output and imports respond in an open, short-run way.
- **On the slide:** the redrawn Fig. 2 below (qualitative, no numbers, ILLUSTRATIVE banner). Beneath it a before / during / after strip with real facts:
  - BEFORE: modelled industrial electricity price for companies without network-charge and tax reductions rose from 17.1 ct/kWh (2020) to 35.9 (2022) [SMARD, vintage of 22 Oct 2026]; direct extra-EU Chapter 72 imports were already falling, 3,337 kt (2021) to 2,697 kt (2022) [Comext].
  - DURING (reporting Oct 2023–Dec 2025, no payments): direct imports 2,089 kt (2023) to 1,464 kt (2025) [Comext]; electricity 16.1 ct/kWh in 2024 and 16.5 in 2025 [SMARD].
  - AFTER (2026): Jan–Aug 2026 direct imports 1,108 kt against 1,028 kt in Jan–Aug 2025, +7.7% in tonnes and −0.7% in value (provisional) [Comext]; electricity 17.3 ct/kWh for Jan–Sep 2026; CBAM certificate prices EUR 75.36, 75.28 and 82.32 per tCO2e for Q1–Q3 2026, Q4 due 4 Jan 2027 [Dataset E]; first payments 2027; no after-effect claim.

- **Limits:** SMARD is a Bundesnetzagentur model, not invoiced prices; it states no consumption band and is not steel-specific; never say "steelmakers paid" or "tripled".
- **Fallback:** a mechanism box with the same strip.

- **On the slide:** the chart below, both lines on the tonnes panel and value on the lower panel. Caption: unit value EUR/t 736 (2019), 1,176 (2022), 1,087 (2025), a product-mix indicator and not a price index [Dataset C]. - **Say:** imports rose after the 2005 launch to the 2007 peak, so we do not claim ETS cut imports. Materiality: direct imports are 8.1% of German Chapter 72 import tonnes in 2024 (6.1% in 2025, 13.4% in 2017); by origin, non-EU steel is 17.3% in 2024. By origin, Jan–Jul 2026 is +6.1% (provisional). Do not quote single months (year-on-year swings run from −44% to +49%).

## 5. CH3 — Conclusion and suggestion (2.5 min)

### S12 · 1.0 min · Conclusion: can strengthen, if conditions hold

- **Message:** The record is consistent with ETS and CBAM supporting a move to cleaner steel, but a measured net German gain, cost parity, delivered tonnes and a fall in CO2 per tonne are not shown.
- **On the slide:** verdict banner: EU ETS + CBAM can strengthen the long-term move to cleaner steel; the net German benefit is unmeasured and conditional. Three columns.
  - CONSISTENT WITH: the carbon-cost comparison now includes embedded emissions [U3] (Policy rule); dated commitments 2021–26 [U10][U12][U13][U14][U15] (Company announcement; large public aid pre-dates CBAM reporting, so not a CBAM response); covered EU ETS emissions about 50% below 2005 in 2024, an EU aggregate with the power sector an important driver [U29] (Observed); OECD: about 10% emissions cut in 2005–12 with no measured harm to regulated firms, earlier period [U30] (Published research).
  - NOT SHOWN: a fall in CO2 per tonne (electric-arc share 29.2% in 1999, 27.7–32% in 2020–25 and 30.5% in 2025; ETS-reported CO2 per tonne of crude steel 0.84 in 2013 and 0.88 in 2025; WV Stahl specific CO2 1.37 in 2019 and 1.39 in 2025); causal size; net German GDP or welfare gain (scenario studies: smaller than sectoral effects, assumption-sensitive [S19]); cheaper green steel; delivered tonnes; tax and net budget effects; paid-CBAM effects (first payments 2027).
  - CONDITIONAL ON: affordable clean power and hydrogen; finance; new low-carbon plants running (first output 2027 or later); verified emissions data; workable administration [U12][U20].
- **Say:** German ETS-reported emissions of iron and steel installations fell about 16% between 2019 and 2025 while crude steel fell about 14%, so the fall follows output; per tonne nothing has moved yet.
- **Label:** mixed, per-item chips; verdict = Team judgement.

### S13 · 1.5 min · Suggestion: keep the framework, change the delivery

- **Message:** Keep a predictable ETS plus CBAM framework, improve how it is delivered, and reconsider only if monitoring shows leakage, losses that exceed benefits, or no verified fall in emissions per tonne.
- **Addressed to:** EU legislators and the Commission (rules); the German government and the emissions-trading authority DEHSt (support, monitoring, retraining).
- **On the slide:** three columns.
  - KEEP: ETS + CBAM with the phase-in to 2034 under current law (a 17 Jul 2026 proposal would stretch it to 2038; undecided) [S9]; climate design kept apart from trade defence such as the steel quota [U6]; proportionate treatment of small importers, since the 50-tonne threshold would exempt 0.87% of embedded emissions (Commission assessment of 30 Sep 2026, limit 1%) [S23].
